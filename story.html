The Story of Kelvin Kipkoech

From the villages of Baringo to a world of possibilities.

One life. Many dreams. Countless possibilities.

Before the dreams, there was a village.

My name is Kelvin Kipkoech, popularly known as Alvin. My story begins in Eldama Ravine, Baringo County, Kenya.

I am a product of humble beginnings, village experiences, cultural values and lessons that cannot always be found in a classroom.

I grew up in a village in Baringo, where life had its own classroom. Before understanding the complexities of the world, I understood the simplicity of village life. I spent part of my childhood living with my grandmother, grazing cows in the fields and along the roads, interacting with friends and learning lessons through everyday experiences.

My grandmother had a way of teaching that I still carry with me. One of her memorable pieces of advice was that not everyone you call a friend is necessarily your friend.

Back then, some lessons sounded like ordinary grandmother advice. Growing older made me understand their meaning.

Those village experiences helped shape the person I am today. They taught me about patience, humility, relationships, resilience and the importance of knowing where I come from.

The school years: Memories beyond the classroom.

My academic journey took me through Esageri Primary School and later Kabimoi High School.

High school was an experience of its own. There were friendships, challenges, unforgettable entertainment, laughter, discipline and moments that still deserve their own documentary. 😂

We had our fair share of critics. Some people misunderstood our generation, but we had our own understanding of friendship, humanity and having a good time.

And yes, somehow, we still managed to study and pass our examinations. Entertainment and education were apparently capable of sharing the same timetable! 😂

Those years taught me that education is not only about passing examinations. It is also about discovering yourself, learning how to coexist with different people and preparing for life beyond the school gates.

My cultural identity: Knowing where I come from.

I am proud of my cultural roots.

My connection to the Lembus community, Chumo identity and Kalenjin heritage is an important part of my story.

I appreciate our language, traditions, history, music, customs and the values passed down through generations.

I believe that knowing where you come from gives you a better understanding of where you want to go.

I do not believe that embracing modern technology requires abandoning traditional identity. We can be technologically advanced, professionally competitive and still proudly connected to our culture.

I carry my roots with pride. They are part of my identity, not something I need to hide.

Education, construction and the future.

My journey eventually brought me to Rift Valley National Polytechnic, where I pursued Building Technology under the Building and Civil Engineering department.

Construction introduced me to another way of thinking: planning, designing, measuring, calculating and transforming ideas into physical structures.

I became interested in building construction, architectural drawings, engineering surveying, substructure, superstructure, site operations and construction management.

I also developed an interest in Archicad and the technical processes behind building projects.

My ambition is to become a competent building technologist, gain practical experience and grow towards professional responsibilities in construction, particularly site supervision.

I believe that every structure begins with an idea, but turning that idea into reality requires knowledge, teamwork, patience and commitment.

Beyond construction: The entrepreneurial side of me.

I have never wanted to limit myself to one field.

My curiosity extends into technology, digital innovation, entrepreneurship, graphic design, website development and artificial intelligence.

I am interested in learning how websites are built, how digital businesses operate and how technology can create opportunities for ordinary people.

This curiosity inspired my interest in Kev Teleworld, a digital brand built around the idea:

Connecting Ideas to the Digital World.

I have explored furniture marketing, digital services, website development, online opportunities, automation and creative branding.

Some ideas are still developing. Others are experiments. Some are simply lessons in progress.

But I believe that learning a new skill today can create an opportunity tomorrow.

I am not afraid of starting small. Every professional was once a beginner.

The person behind the ambitions.

I am curious, creative, sometimes humorous, sometimes quiet and occasionally the person with seventeen ideas before breakfast. 😂

I enjoy football, especially Manchester United. GGMU!

I appreciate music across different genres, from country and gospel to amapiano, hip-hop, old-school classics and Kenyan music, particularly music in my mother tongue.

I enjoy storytelling, graphic design, social media creativity and conversations about technology, business, culture and life.

I can be serious about my ambitions and still find something funny in an ordinary situation.

I enjoy learning, asking questions, experimenting with ideas and finding alternative ways of doing things.

I am also someone who reflects on past decisions. I know that personal growth involves learning, accepting mistakes and becoming more intentional about the future.

I do not claim to have everything figured out. I am still discovering my strengths, improving my weaknesses and learning how to turn ambition into actual results.

Leadership and community.

I believe that progress should not be an individual achievement alone.

Throughout my experiences, I have taken an interest in student leadership, community activities, group organisation and bringing people together.

My involvement in student and community spaces has helped me appreciate the importance of communication, responsibility, teamwork and listening to other people's perspectives.

I believe leadership is not simply about having a title. It is about being available, understanding people's concerns, taking responsibility and contributing something meaningful.

I want to be part of environments where people can share ideas, develop their abilities and work towards common goals.

My philosophy: Still becoming.

I come from a place where ordinary experiences taught me extraordinary lessons.

I have dreams bigger than my current circumstances, interests that extend beyond my classroom and ambitions that continue to evolve.

I understand that the journey towards success is not always straightforward. There are things to learn, skills to develop, opportunities to pursue and responsibilities to take seriously.

I am not writing this story as someone who has already reached the destination.

I am writing it as someone who is still on the journey.

From village experiences in Baringo to technical education, from cultural identity to digital innovation, from childhood dreams to professional ambitions, every chapter contributes something to the person I am becoming.

I want to build things, create opportunities, learn continuously and leave something meaningful behind.

I am Kelvin Kipkoech.

I am Alvin.

A son of Baringo. A proud Lembus. A Building Technology student. An aspiring entrepreneur. A curious mind. A work in progress.

And if there is one thing you should know about me, it is this:

I may not have everything today, but I have ideas to explore, skills to develop, a story worth telling and a future worth working for.
